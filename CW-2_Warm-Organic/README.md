# CW-2 · Warm-Organic

## 1. One-line definition
A tactile, human web aesthetic of **earth, grain and hand**: unbleached-paper grounds, soft or wonky serifs, irregular pebble and leaf shapes, paper grain, hand-drawn marks and real people at work. It is used by bakeries, coffee and food brands, farms, wellness and skincare, NGOs and sustainability or craft brands that want to feel honest, close and alive.

## 2. Position on the map

| Axis | Score | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Classical ↔ Expressive | **3 / 5** (middle) | The structure stays readable and orderly (classical), but shapes, hand-drawn marks and wonky type add personality (expressive). |
| Cool ↔ Warm | **5 / 5** (Warm) | The warmest folder: earthy hues, skin, food, hands, handwriting. |
| Density | **2.5 / 5** | Airier than editorial, denser than luxury. Stories, recipes and product info all need room. |
| Motion | **2.5 / 5** | Gentle: breathing blob morphs, strokes that draw on, small springy stamps. Nothing fast or mechanical. |
| Ornament | **4 / 5** | Grain, illustration, stamps, doodles, patterns from the product (furrows, coffee beans, leaves). |
| Contrast | **2 / 5** | Soft value contrast (brown on oat, cream on forest green). Accents are muted, never neon. |

## 3. Best-fit purposes

| Purpose | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|

| Personal | ➖ | Chefs, herbalists, yoga teachers. |

## 4. Visual DNA

### Typography
- **Display**: soft, slightly irregular serifs with ink-trap, "cooked" or wonky details, set medium-light (350–450) and fairly tight.
  - Commercial: Recoleta, Cooper, GT Alpina, Ogg, Gazpacho, Roslindale, Tobias, PP Editorial Old, Reckless Neue, Kalice, Migra. Hand-cut or custom wordmarks are common (Agrumea's swashed "Farm" script, MontRural's drawn monogram).
  - **Free (Google Fonts)**: **Fraunces** with `"SOFT" 100, "WONK" 1` (the single best free choice), Young Serif, Gloock, DM Serif Display, Bagel Fat One (playful), Gelasio, Literata.
- **Text/UI**: a friendly humanist or geometric sans at 17–19 px: DM Sans, Figtree, Work Sans, Instrument Sans, Nunito Sans. Or a typewriter mono for an apothecary tone (Helias uses a Courier-like mono for product copy): Courier Prime, IBM Plex Mono.
- **Handwriting** as accent only (≤5 notes per page): Caveat, Kalam, Gochi Hand, or better, a scan of a real person's handwriting.
- Caps only lightly tracked (0.06–0.1 em). Wide tracking reads as luxury, not warmth.

### Colour

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flour | `#F3EBDD` | Page ground (unbleached paper) |
| Oat | `#EADFCB` | Panels, bands |
| Soot | `#2A2119` | Text, 13.3:1 on Flour |
| Rye | `#5A3A22` | Secondary text and headings, 8.6:1 |
| Crust | `#B4602B` / text `#9A4A1E` | Signature accent / AA text version (5.3:1) |
| Sage | `#7C8B63` / text `#4F5B3C` | Plant accent / AA text version (6.1:1) |
| Butter | `#E9C46A` | Buttons, stamps, highlights (with Soot text, 9.5:1) |
| Field | `#2F3A2A` | Dark sections, footer |

Logic: colours are **sampled from the product and its place**: crust, flour, leaf, soil, terracotta, coffee. One saturated "hero" product colour is allowed (The Olly's tomato-red frame, Wild Souls' tangerine set, Agrumea's blood orange, Delta's coffee brown). Avoid pure white and pure black.

### Layout and grid
- 12-column grid, but **things sit slightly off-true**: cards rotated ±1–2°, stickers and stamps overlapping edges, notes in the margins.
- Rounded containers (20–40 px radius), **pebble/blob masks** for imagery, curved section dividers, and arched or domed shapes (MontRural's tab-notch into its hero photo; Helias' huge organic olive curve splitting the hero).
- Content-led sections: process ("soil to slice", "bean to cup"), origin map, people, recipes, journal.
- Product shown as an isolated cut-out on a coloured field (The Olly's loaf, Agrumea's floating jars, Wild Souls' jar line-up on a painted paper backdrop).

### Imagery and art direction
- **Human photography**: hands kneading, farmers in fields, close-ups of crumbs, steam, soil, natural daylight, honest imperfection.
- Set-built backdrops: painted paper shapes, cut-out cacti and terracotta pots (Wild Souls), linen and wood.
- Illustration: botanical line drawings, watercolour leaves (MontRural's ivy framing the whole site), hand-drawn icons, archival labels and maps.
- Texture everywhere: paper grain, kraft, risograph-ish dots, linocut edges.

### Motion and interaction
- Breathing `border-radius` blob morph (8–12 s loop), SVG strokes that draw on with `stroke-dashoffset` as they enter, rotating text badges ("100% PURE & NATURAL", "NO-TILL · HERITAGE GRAIN").
- Gentle spring (≤6% overshoot) on stamps, add-to-basket and buttons. Floating product cut-outs with soft parallax (Agrumea's jars).
- Interactive storytelling tied to the product: a seasonal wheel, origin map, coffee-roast slider, ingredient explorer.
- Hover: hand-drawn underline swipes, a small wobble rotation, stickers peeling.

### Micro-details
- Rubber-stamp marks (batch numbers, "SOLD OUT", certifications), torn-paper or deckled edges, dashed "stitch" borders.
- Offset "sticker" shadows (`3px 3px 0 ink`) instead of blurred drop-shadows on buttons.
- Handwritten marginalia with drawn arrows, written by a named person ("a note from Tom").
- SVG `feTurbulence` grain at 10–16% multiply over everything.
- Tiny line icons drawn with the same stroke width as the illustrations (1.5–2 px, round caps).

## 5. Why it works

**Warm colour, approachability and arousal.** Valdez & Mehrabian (1994, *Effects of color on emotions*) found pleasure is driven mostly by brightness and arousal by saturation. Warm, medium-bright, lower-saturation earth tones feel pleasant without agitation. Labrecque & Milne (2012) tie colour to brand personality, with browns and greens associated with ruggedness, nature and sincerity. See also the general colour and aesthetics literature in `00_Research/academic-findings.md`.

**Curves are liked.** Bar & Neta (2006, *Humans prefer curved visual objects*, Psychological Science 17(8)) showed people prefer curved over sharp-angled objects, possibly because sharp angles signal threat. Westerman et al. (2012, *Product design: Preference for rounded versus angular design elements*, Psychology & Marketing 29(8)) replicated this for products. This underpins the pebble masks, rounded containers and soft serifs.

**Nature imagery restores attention.** Biophilic design theory (Kellert, Heerwagen & Mador, 2008, *Biophilic Design*; Browning, Ryan & Clancy, 2014, *14 Patterns of Biophilic Design*) and Attention Restoration Theory (Kaplan, 1995, *The restorative benefits of nature*, J. Environmental Psychology 15(3)) suggest that natural forms, materials and views reduce stress and restore attention. Ulrich (1984, *View through a window may influence recovery from surgery*, Science 224) is the classic evidence. Taylor et al. (2011, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience) found mid-complexity natural fractals reduce physiological stress, a plausible basis for grain, leaf and furrow patterns used at low contrast.

**Handmade signals love and authenticity.** Fuchs, Schreier & van Osselaer (2015, *The handmade effect: What's love got to do with it?*, J. Marketing 79(2)) showed handmade products are seen to "contain love" and are preferred, especially as gifts. Schroll, Schnurr & Grewal (2018, *Humanizing products with handwritten typefaces*, J. Consumer Research 45(3)) found handwritten typefaces create a sense of human presence and raise product evaluations, which is why marginalia and hand-drawn marks work (when used sparingly).

**Typeface personality.** Henderson, Giese & Cote (2004) found "natural" typefaces (organic, slightly irregular, rounded) rate as warm and engaging. Brumberger (2003) shows readers assign consistent personas (friendly, elegant, direct) to typefaces. Velasco et al. (2015, *The taste of typeface*, i-Perception 6(4)) found rounder typefaces are associated with sweeter tastes, relevant for food brands.

**Fluency balance.** Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman (2004) predict liking for easy-to-process layouts. This style keeps the structure fluent (clear grid, big readable text) and adds novelty only at the surface (texture, doodles), which is the "most advanced yet acceptable" balance.

**What juries reward.** Awwwards winners in this space (Wild Souls SOTD 2021, Helias SOTD 2020, Ceremony Coffee SOTD 2018, The Olly HM 2024, Agrumea Farm, MontRural and Delta Cafés HMs 2026) combine **strong art direction of the product with a story**: heritage (Delta's 60 years, The Olly's 1956 bakery), place (Sicily, Penedès) or process. They pair this with smooth, playful but restrained interaction. Generic "eco" templates with leaf icons and stock salads do not place.

## 6. Uniqueness levers
1. **Calendar or process as navigation**: a seasonal wheel, harvest timeline or "bean to cup" journey that organises the content.
2. **Ledger, label and stamp language**: present the daily menu, batch or roast as a ruled ledger, apothecary label or rubber-stamped card.
3. **Named-human marginalia**: handwritten notes signed by the actual baker, farmer or founder, with drawn arrows.
4. **Patterns from the product**: furrow lines, crumb texture, coffee-bean or leaf repeats generated in SVG, never generic leaves.
5. **Set-built photography**: product on painted paper, cut-outs, real props, rather than lifestyle stock.
6. **Breathing shapes**: pebble masks that slowly morph, like dough proving or a leaf moving.
7. **Origin map**: hand-drawn map of fields, farms or suppliers with distances ("2 km away").
8. **Material swap per section**: kraft, linen, ceramic glaze and wood as section grounds.
9. **One bold product colour** against the earth palette (The Olly's full red frame).
10. **Honest numbers**: "140 loaves, then we stop", worm counts, carbon or water per item. Specific facts replace "eco-friendly".

## 7. Clichés and pitfalls
- Green leaf icons, "eco-friendly", "sustainable", "natural goodness" headlines and stock photos of salads and smiling yoga people. This is greenwashing aesthetics, and audiences now distrust it.
- Mint-green-plus-white Shopify template with a centred hero and three benefit icons.
- Overusing script fonts (Pacifico, Dancing Script) for headlines; handwriting for body text.
- Too many blobs everywhere (2020 "blobby SaaS" look); random blob shapes that don't relate to the product.
- Beige-on-beige with no contrast, which fails WCAG and looks muddy.
- Fake texture: low-res paper JPGs that tile visibly and slow the page.
- Earth palette with *nothing* human: no faces, hands or real process means no warmth.
- AI tell-tales: "Nourish your body, nurture the planet", wavy SVG dividers between every section, identical rounded cards with emoji icons.

## 8. Accessibility and performance
- Earth palettes fail contrast easily. Keep body text ≥ 7:1 if possible (Soot on Flour is 13.3:1), use the darker "text" versions of accents (`#9A4A1E`, `#4F5B3C`), and never put small text on Sage `#7C8B63` (only 4.3:1 with Soot).
- Handwritten fonts: only for short notes ≥ 20 px; never for essential information without a plain-text equivalent.
- Grain overlays: `pointer-events:none`, ≤ 16% opacity, and check that text contrast still passes underneath.
- Blob morph and rotating badges must stop under `prefers-reduced-motion`; keep the draw-on strokes visible with no animation.
- Interactive widgets (seasonal wheel, map) need keyboard support (arrow keys, Enter), `aria-live` updates and button roles.
- Use SVG (vector) for illustrations and noise (tiny data URIs) instead of large texture images; photos in AVIF/WebP with art-directed `srcset`.
- Photos of people need meaningful alt text ("Ada, the farmer, holding rye at harvest"), not "smiling woman".

## 9. Neighbouring styles and hybrids
- **CW-1 Luxury-Refined**: "Quiet luxury" or wabi-sabi hybrid for premium skincare (Aesop-like), ryokans and fine dining: fewer doodles, more whitespace, serif kept but grain retained (MontRural sits on this boundary).
- **EW-1 Playful-Illustrative**: add mascots, bright colours and bouncy motion for snack and drink DTC brands (Wild Souls leans this way with tangerine and lilac paper shapes).
- **EW-2 Retro-Nostalgic**: heritage labels, risograph textures and 70s type for cafés, breweries and bakeries with history (The Olly, Delta's 1961 archive).
- **CC-1 Swiss-Editorial**: NGO annual reports and sustainability data: an editorial grid with warm palette and photography.
- **EC-1 Immersive**: 3D product storytelling (Agrumea's Three.js jars, Delta's coffee-bean particles) inside an organic frame.
